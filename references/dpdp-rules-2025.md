# Digital Personal Data Protection Rules, 2025 (DPDP Rules)

**Verified 2026-09-14** — cross-checked directly against the official Gazette notification text (not a secondary summary): G.S.R. 846(E), Ministry of Electronics & Information Technology, dated 13 November 2025.

Source: MeitY Notification G.S.R. 846(E), New Delhi, dated **13 November 2025**, made under sub-sections (1) and (2) of section 40 of the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023. Official: meity.gov.in "Digital Personal Data Protection Rules 2025" (Gazette PDF), also mirrored at dpdpa.com/DPDP_Rules_2025_English_only.pdf. Note: a lot of press/secondary coverage cites "14 November 2025" as the notification date — that's the date it became widely publicized, but the Gazette instrument itself is dated and signed **13 November 2025**; treat 13 Nov 2025 as the authoritative date if a spec or doc cites a specific date.

**These Rules are a separate, subordinate legal instrument from the DPDP Act, 2023 itself** (see `dpdp-act-2023.md`). The Act sets out the rights/obligations skeleton (consent, notice, breach duty, SDF status, etc.); these Rules operationalize it with the actual mechanics — notice content, retention periods, grievance timelines, breach-notification steps, Consent Manager registration, children's/disability verifiable consent, Significant Data Fiduciary specifics, and Data Protection Board procedure. **If a spec cites "the DPDP Act" for a specific number (a retention period, a grievance SLA, a 72-hour breach window) — that number actually lives in these Rules, not the Act.** Flag if a spec doesn't distinguish the two, since the Act alone doesn't contain those figures.

## Rule 1(2)-(4) — Phased commencement (read this before citing any obligation below)
- **Rules 1, 2, and 17–21** (Data Protection Board constitution, governance, meeting/inquiry procedure, digital-office functioning, staff terms) — in force immediately, from 13 November 2025.
- **Rule 4** (Consent Manager registration & obligations, incl. First Schedule) — in force **13 November 2026** (one year after Gazette publication).
- **Rules 3, 5–16, 22, 23** — in force **13 May 2027** (eighteen months after Gazette publication). This block includes Rule 6 (security safeguards), Rule 7 (breach intimation), **Rule 8 (retention — see below)**, Rule 9 (DPO/contact info), Rules 10–11 (children's/disability consent), Rule 13 (SDF additional obligations), **Rule 14 (Data Principal rights incl. grievance timeline — see below)**, Rule 15 (cross-border transfer conditions), Rule 16 (research/archival exemption).
- **Practical read for POP right now (Sep 2026):** the retention (Rule 8(3)) and grievance-SLA (Rule 14(3)) obligations quoted below are drafted, dated, and real — but **not yet legally binding**; they become operative on 13 May 2027. Don't tell a partner/bank "the DPDP Rules already require X" for these specific clauses today. Do flag in a review that a spec should be *designed* to meet them well ahead of that date, since retrofitting retention/grievance infra late is expensive.

## Rule 8(3) — Retain personal data + associated traffic data + logs for a minimum of 1 year
Exact text: *"a Data Fiduciary shall retain, in respect of any processing of personal data undertaken by it or on its behalf by a Data Processor, such personal data, associated traffic data and other logs of the processing for a minimum period of one year from the date of such processing, for the purposes as specified in the Seventh Schedule, after which the Data Fiduciary shall cause such personal data and logs to be erased, unless further retention is required for compliance with any other law for the time being in force or notified by the Government."*

Do not confuse this with **Rule 8(1)-(2)**'s separate erasure-on-inactivity regime, which only applies to large e-commerce/online-gaming/social-media platforms (Third Schedule thresholds: ≥2 crore users for e-commerce/social media, ≥50 lakh for gaming) — those must erase most personal data **3 years** after a Data Principal last engages, with a mandatory 48-hour pre-erasure warning. The two rules run in parallel and serve different purposes: Rule 8(3)'s 1-year floor is about keeping enough data/logs around for breach investigation and Seventh-Schedule government-access purposes; the Third Schedule's 3-year rule is about *not* hoarding data indefinitely once a large-platform user goes dark.

## Rule 14(3) — Grievance redressal within a reasonable period not exceeding 90 days
Exact text: *"Every Data Fiduciary and Consent Manager shall prominently publish on its website or app, or both, as the case may be, within a reasonable period not exceeding ninety days under its grievance redressal system for responding to the grievances of Data Principals and shall, for ensuring the effectiveness of the system in responding within such period, implement appropriate technical and organisational measures."*

## Rule 7 — Breach notification mechanics (ties to DPDP Act s.8(6))
To the Board: an immediate "without delay" initial description (nature/extent/timing/location/likely impact), followed by a detailed report within **72 hours** of becoming aware (or a longer Board-approved window on written request) covering updated facts, cause, mitigation, findings on who caused it, remedial steps, and a report on Data Principal intimations. To each affected Data Principal: "without delay," via their registered user account/contact channel, covering the breach description, likely consequences to them, mitigation steps taken, safety steps they should take, and a contact for queries.

## Rule 6(1)(e) — Security-incident log retention (also 1 year, but a narrower/different trigger than Rule 8(3))
As part of the general "reasonable security safeguards" obligation, logs and personal data tied to detecting/investigating unauthorised access must be retained "for a period of one year, unless compliance with any law for the time being in force requires otherwise." Cite Rule 6(1)(e) specifically for this security-logging duty — don't conflate it with the general Rule 8(3) retention floor even though both land on "one year."

## Rule 10 — Verifiable parental consent for a child's data
Data Fiduciary must obtain verifiable consent of the parent before processing a child's personal data, with due diligence to confirm the "parent" is an identifiable adult (18+) — either via identity/age details already held, or voluntarily provided (directly, or via a virtual token from an authorised entity / Digital Locker provider).
