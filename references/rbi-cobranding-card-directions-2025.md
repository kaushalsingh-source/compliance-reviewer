# RBI (Commercial Banks – Credit Cards and Debit Cards: Issuance and Conduct) Directions, 2025

Source: RBI/DOR/2025-26/155, issued 28 November 2025. Official: https://www.rbi.org.in/scripts/BS_ViewMasDirections.aspx?id=13155 (PDF: https://rbidocs.rbi.org.in/rdocs/notification/PDFs/155MD.PDF) — fetched and cross-checked 2026-08-21. **This replaced the 2022 Master Direction on Credit Card and Debit Card Issuance and Conduct (Para 93 repeals the old framework for commercial banks).** If any doc under review still cites the "2022 Master Direction," that version is stale — flag it.

## Para 8 — Board-approved policy
Every card-issuer must maintain a well-documented, Board-approved policy for card issuance and conduct, published on its website.

## Paras 11(1)-(2), 92 — KFS & MITC disclosure
A one-page Key Fact Statement must accompany every credit card application, covering rate of interest and quantum of charges (11(1)). Most Important Terms & Conditions (MITC) must be provided at onboarding, preferably in English, Hindi, and the local language, and again whenever a term is modified (11(2)). Chapter VII / Para 92 requires MITC disclosure at: marketing stage, application, welcome kit, billing statements, and any T&C change.

## Para 11(4) — Unsolicited card issuance ban
Issuing or upgrading a card without the customer's request is strictly prohibited. If it happens, charges must be reversed immediately and the issuer pays the cardholder twice the value of the reversed charges as penalty.

## Para 11(6) — OTP-based activation
If a card sits inactive for more than 30 days, the issuer must seek OTP-based consent before activation. If still not activated, the card must be closed within 7 working days at no cost to the customer.

## Paras 19-20 — Closure timelines & penalty
A closure request must be honored within 7 working days of dues being settled; missing that deadline costs the issuer ₹500 per calendar day of delay, payable to the cardholder (Para 19). Cards unused for over a year get a 30-day notice before automatic closure (Para 20).

## Paras 55, 58, 59, 60, 61, 62 — Co-branding specific
- **Para 55**: cards must explicitly disclose the co-branding arrangement; the card-issuer (bank) must be clearly identified in all marketing material — the co-branding partner (CBP) cannot present itself as the product owner.
- **Para 58**: the Board-approved policy must specifically address reputation risk from the co-branding arrangement; revenue-sharing between issuer and CBP must be disclosed to the cardholder and displayed on the issuer's website.
- **Para 59**: if the CBP is itself a financial entity, it needs its own regulator's approval for the co-branding arrangement, and due diligence applies.
- **Para 60**: the card-issuer is liable for the CBP's performance — including timely delivery of any cashback/discounts/offers the CBP advertised.
- **Para 61**: CBP's role is restricted to marketing/distribution of the card and enabling access to the CBP's own goods/services — nothing else.
- **Para 62**: CBP cannot access transaction information under any circumstance, regardless of what other services it provides. Post-issuance, it has no role in processes or controls relating to the co-branded card. Any transaction data shown on a CBP platform must be encrypted and visible only to the cardholder — the CBP itself neither accesses nor stores it.

## Para 83, 85 — Grievance redressal
Grievance redressal officer contact details must be publicized, including on billing statements (83). Unresolved complaints after 30 days can escalate to the RBI Ombudsman, which can award compensation for financial loss, harassment, or mental anguish (85).
