# RBI (Digital Lending) Directions, 2025

Source: RBI/2025-26/36, DOR.STR.REC.19/21.07.001/2025-26, effective 8 May 2025 (Para 6 multi-lender provisions effective 1 Nov 2025; Para 17 CIMS reporting effective 15 Jun 2025). Official notification: https://www.rbi.org.in/scripts/NotificationUser.aspx?Id=12848 — fetched and cross-checked 2026-08-21. This supersedes the repealed 2022 Digital Lending Guidelines circular; if any other document still cites the 2022 circular, that's itself worth flagging.

## Para 5 — LSP due diligence
Regulated Entity (RE) must conduct enhanced due diligence before engaging a Lending Service Provider (LSP), evaluating technical capabilities and robustness of data privacy policies/storage systems (5.ii), with periodic review of LSP conduct (5.iii-iv). Outsourcing to an LSP never dilutes or absolves the RE's own obligations under these Directions (5.vii) — the RE is on the hook regardless of what the LSP/TSP does.

## Para 6 — Multi-lender / co-lending arrangements (effective 1 Nov 2025)
An LSP working with multiple REs must show the borrower a digital view of all matching loan offers, naming unmatched lenders too (6.i), using a consistent matching approach for similarly-placed borrowers (6.ii). Each offer display must show RE name(s), amount, tenor, APR, monthly repayment, penal charges, and a KFS link enabling fair comparison (6.iii). Content must be unbiased/objective — no dark patterns steering toward one lender (6.iv).

## Para 8 — Key Fact Statement (KFS)
KFS must be provided per RBI circular DOR.STR.REC.13/13.03.00/2024-25 (8.i). KFS, sanction letter, and privacy policy must be digitally signed and delivered automatically to the borrower's verified email/SMS (8.iii).

## Para 9 — Disbursement & repayment flow-of-funds
Disbursement must always land in the borrower's own bank account (9.i) — exceptions only for statutory mandates, co-lending, or direct end-use disbursal. Repayment must be executed by the borrower directly into the RE's account (9.ii); the flow of funds between borrower and RE must never be controlled, directly or indirectly, by a third party (9.iii). LSP fees/charges are paid by the RE directly — never passed through or charged to the borrower (9.iv).

## Para 10 — Cooling-off / look-up period
Borrower must get an explicit option to exit the loan within a cooling-off period by repaying principal + proportionate APR, with no penalty (10.i). Minimum period is one day; RE's board sets the actual duration. A reasonable one-time processing fee may be retained on exit, but must be disclosed upfront in the KFS (10.ii).

## Paras 12-13 — Data collection, storage, consent
Data collection must be need-based, with prior explicit consent and an audit trail (12.i). Digital Lending Apps (DLAs) must not access mobile file/media, contact list, or call logs; only camera, microphone, and location for KYC purposes may take one-time consent (12.i). Borrowers must be able to grant/deny consent per data category, restrict third-party disclosure, and revoke consent (12.ii). LSPs may store only minimal data (name, address, contact) needed for their operations (13.i). No biometric data may be stored/collected unless separate statutory guidelines permit it (13.iii). All data must be stored on servers located within India (13.iv).

## Para 11 — Grievance redressal
RE and LSP must each designate a nodal grievance redressal officer with contact details prominently displayed (11.i-ii), with complaint facilities available on the DLA and website (11.iii). Unresolved complaints after 30 days escalate to RBI's Complaint Management System under the Reserve Bank-Integrated Ombudsman Scheme (11.iv).

## Para 17 — CIMS reporting
RE must report every DLA on RBI's CIMS portal (format per Annex I): DLA name, owner, platform, grievance officer, RE website link (17.i), kept updated as DLAs are deployed/retired (17.ii). Chief Compliance Officer must certify DLA compliance (17.iii-iv). Original deadline was 15 June 2025.
